In this section, we discuss the following:
\begin{itemize}
\item
    the expression of the magnetic dipole moment of a particle
    in terms of the intrinsic spin of the particle,
\item
    how the Pauli-Schrödinger equation arises through canonical quantization
    of the Hamiltonian of an electrically charged particle,
    possibly with non-zero intrinsic spin, inside an electromagnetic field, 
\item
    how the Pauli-Schrödinger equation can be used to explain the observations
    of the Stern-Gerlach experiment, and
\item
    how the Pauli-Schrödinger equation can be regarded as a non-relativistic limit
    of the Dirac equation.
\end{itemize}

\vskip 0.5cm
\section{The magnetic dipole moment of a particle is proportional to its intrinsic spin}

          %%%%% ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ %%%%%

The \,\textbf{magnetic dipole moment}\, \,$\boldsymbol{\mu}_{S}$\,
of a Dirac particle (i.e., elementary particle of intrinsic spin $\frac{1}{2}$)
% of rest mass \,$m$,\, electric charge \,$q$,\, and
% intrinsic spin \,$\boldsymbol{S}$\,
is given by:
\begin{equation*}
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{S}
\; = \;
    g \cdot \dfrac{q}{2m} \cdot \mathbf{S}\,,
\end{equation*}
where
\begin{itemize}
\item
    $q$\, is the electric charge of the particle (for an electron, $q = -e$),
\item
    $m$\, is the rest mass of the particle,
\item
    $\mathbf{S}$\, is the spin angular momentum vector operator, and
\item
    $g$ is the dimensionless Landé $g$-factor (or spin $g$-factor), which is approximately $2$ for elementary fermions like electrons, muons, and quarks.
\end{itemize}
In this section, we explain the above classical formula (or definition)
of the magnetic dipole moment of a Dirac particle.

          %%%%% ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ %%%%%

\vskip 0.5cm
\subsection{Classical charged particle moving in circular orbit: \,$\boldsymbol{\mu}_{L} = \dfrac{q}{2m}\mathbf{L}$}

          %%%%% ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ %%%%%

\noindent
Consider an elementary particle of rest mass \,$m > 0$\, and
electric charge \,$q \in \Re\,\backslash\{\,0\,\}$\,
moving in a circular trajectory of radius \,$r > 0$\,
with constant speed \,$v > 0$.\,
Classically, the particle behaves like a magnetic dipole,
in the sense that it behaves electromagnetically
as a fictitious ensemble of two ``opposite magnetic charges''
separated by a small distance.

\vskip 0.5cm
\begin{lemma}[Classical magnetic dipole moment in terms of orbital angular momentum]
\mbox{}
\vskip 0.02cm
\noindent
The (classical) magnetic dipole moment
\,$\boldsymbol{\mu}_{L}$\,
of the aforementioned particle can be expressed
in terms of the (classical) orbital angular moment \,$\mathbf{L}$\,
of the particle as follows:
\begin{equation*}
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{L}
\;\; = \;\;
    \dfrac{q}{2m}
    \cdot
    \mathbf{L}
\end{equation*}
\end{lemma}
\proof
The moving charge creates a \textit{current loop},
where the current \,$I$\, flowing through the loop is given by:
\begin{equation*}
I
\;\; = \;\;
    \left(\!\begin{array}{c}
        \textnormal{charge}
        \\
        \textnormal{per unit time}
        \end{array}\!\right)
\;\; = \;\;
    \dfrac{q}{2\pi\,r/v}
\;\; = \;\;
    \dfrac{q\,v}{2\pi\,r}\,,
\end{equation*}
while the area enclosed by the (circular) loop is:
\begin{equation*}
A
\;\; = \;\;
    \pi \, r^{2}
\end{equation*}
The (classical) magnetic dipole moment
\,$\boldsymbol{\mu}_{L}$\,
of the current loop is defined as follows:
\begin{equation*}
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{L}
\;\; := \;\;
    I \cdot \boldsymbol{A}
\;\; = \;\;
    \left(\,\dfrac{q\,v}{2\pi\,r}\,\right)
    \left(\,\pi \, r^{2}\,\right)
    \cdot
    \mathbf{e}_{\perp}
\;\; = \;\;
    \dfrac{q\,r\,v}{2}
    \cdot
    \mathbf{e}_{\perp}\,,
\end{equation*}
where
\,$\mathbf{e}_{\perp}$\,
is the unit vector obtained from the flow direction of the current via the right-hand rule, and
\,$\mathbf{A} \,:=\, A \cdot \mathbf{e}_{\perp}$\, is the area vector.
On the other hand, the orbital angular momentum \,$\mathbf{L}$\,
of the particle is:
\begin{equation*}
\mathbf{L}
\;\; = \;\;
    \mathbf{r}
    \times
    \left(\,m\cdot\mathbf{v}\,\right)
\;\; = \;\;
    m\,r\,v
    \cdot
    \mathbf{e}_{\perp}\,,
\end{equation*}
where
\,$\mathbf{r}$\,
is the instantaneous position vector of the particle,
and
\,$\mathbf{v}$\,
is its instantaneous velocity vector.
We thus see that
\begin{equation*}
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{L}
\;\; := \;\;
    I \cdot \boldsymbol{A}
\;\; = \;\;
    \left(\,\dfrac{q\,v}{2\pi\,r}\,\right)
    \left(\,\pi \, r^{2}\,\right)
    \cdot
    \mathbf{e}_{\perp}
\;\; = \;\;
    \dfrac{q\,r\,v}{2}
    \cdot
    \mathbf{e}_{\perp}
\;\; = \;\;
    \dfrac{q}{2m}
    \cdot
    \mathbf{L}\,,
\end{equation*}
as required.
\qed

\vskip 0.5cm
\subsection{Hamiltonian of a classical charged particle in an electromagnetic field}

          %%%%% ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ %%%%%

\begin{definition}[Lorentz force]
\mbox{}
\vskip 0.02cm
\noindent
The Lorentz forece \,$\mathbf{F}$\,
experienced by a classical particle
of rest mass \,$m$\, and electric charge \,$q$\,
inside an electromagnetic field described by
the electric scalar potential \,$\phi$\, and
magnetic vector potential \,$\mathbf{A}$:\,
\begin{equation*}
\mathbf{F}
\;\; = \;\;
    q \cdot
    \left[\,
        \mathbf{E}
        \, \overset{{\color{white}.}}{+} \,
        \mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{B} 
        \,\right]
\;\; = \;\;
    q \cdot
    \left[\,
        -\,\nabla\phi
        - \dfrac{\partial\mathbf{A}}{\partial t}
        + \mathbf{v} \times (\nabla \times \mathbf{A})
        \,\right],
\end{equation*}
where
\,$\mathbf{E} \,=\, -\,\nabla\phi - \dfrac{\partial\mathbf{A}}{\partial t}$\,
is the electric field and
\,$\mathbf{B} \,=\, \nabla \times \mathbf{A}$\,
is the magnetic field.
\end{definition}

\vskip 0.5cm
\begin{lemma}[Lagrangian and Hamiltonian associated with the Lorentz force]
\mbox{}
\vskip 0.02cm
\noindent
The Lagrangian
\,$\mathscr{L}(t, \mathbf{r}, \mathbf{v})$\,
and the Hamiltonian
\,$\mathscr{H}(t, \mathbf{r}, \mathbf{v})$\,
associated with the Lorentz force
\begin{equation*}
\mathbf{F}
\, = \,
    q \cdot
    \left[\,
        -\,\nabla\phi
        - \dfrac{\partial\mathbf{A}}{\partial t}
        + \mathbf{v} \times (\nabla \times \mathbf{A})
        \,\right]
\end{equation*}
are respectively given by:
\begin{eqnarray*}
\mathscr{L}(t, \mathbf{r}, \mathbf{v})
& = &
    \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\cdot\Vert\,\mathbf{v}\,\Vert^2
    \, - \,
    q\cdot\phi(t,\mathbf{r})
    \, + \,
    q\cdot\mathbf{v} \bullet \mathbf{A}(t,\mathbf{r})
\\
\mathscr{H}(t, \mathbf{r}, \mathbf{v})
& = &
    \dfrac{1}{2m}\,\left\Vert\;
        \mathbf{p}(t,\mathbf{r},\mathbf{v})
        \, \overset{{\color{white}.}}{-} \,
        q\cdot\mathbf{A}(t,\mathbf{r})
        \,\right\Vert^{2}
    \, + \,
    q\cdot\phi(t,\mathbf{r})\,,
\end{eqnarray*}
where
\,$\mathbf{p} \,=\, \mathbf{p}(t,\mathbf{r},\mathbf{v})$\,
is the canonical momentum vector,
which is componentwise defined by
\;$p_{i} \,:=\, \dfrac{\partial\mathscr{L}}{\partial v_{i}}$,\;
and thus is equivalently defined (vectorially) by:
\begin{equation*}
\mathbf{p}
\;\; = \;\;
    \mathbf{p}(t,\mathbf{r},\mathbf{v})
\;\; = \;\;
    m\cdot\mathbf{v}
    \, + \,
    q\cdot\mathbf{A}(t,\mathbf{r})
\end{equation*}
\end{lemma}
\proof
We first show that the Euler-Lagrange equations
imply the explicit form of the Lorentz force.
Recall that the Euler-Lagrange equations are:
\begin{equation*}
\dfrac{\d}{\d\,t}\!\left(\,\dfrac{\partial\mathscr{L}}{\partial v_{i}}\,\right)
\, - \,
\dfrac{\partial\mathscr{L}}{\partial\,x_{i}}
\;\; = \;\;
    0
\end{equation*}
Next, we compute the individual terms occurring in the Euler-Lagrange equations.
\begin{equation*}
\dfrac{\partial\mathscr{L}}{\partial v_{i}}
\;\; = \;\;
    \dfrac{\partial}{\partial v_{i}}\!
    \left(\,
        \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\cdot\Vert\,\mathbf{v}\,\Vert^2
        \, - \,
        q\cdot\phi(t,\mathbf{r})
        \, + \,
        q\cdot\mathbf{v} \bullet \mathbf{A}(t,\mathbf{r})
        \,\right)
\;\; = \;\;
    m\,v_{i}
    \, + \,
    q\,A_{i}
\end{equation*}
\begin{eqnarray*}
\dfrac{\d}{\d t}\!\left(\,\dfrac{\partial\mathscr{L}}{\partial v_{i}}\,\right)
& = &
    \dfrac{\d}{\d t}\!\left(\,
        m\,v_{i}
        \, \overset{{\color{white}.}}{+} \,
        q\,A_{i}
        \,\right)
\;\; = \;\;
    m\,\dot{v}_{i}
    \, + \,
    q\cdot\!\left(\,
        \dfrac{\partial A_{i}}{\partial t}
        \, + \,
        \overset{3}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}\;
        \dfrac{\partial A_{i}}{\partial x_{k}}
        \dfrac{\partial x_{k}}{\partial t}
        \,\right)
\;\; = \;\;
    m\,\dot{v}_{i}
    \, + \,
    q\cdot\dfrac{\partial A_{i}}{\partial t}
    \, + \,
    q\cdot
    \overset{3}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}\;
    v_{k}
    \dfrac{\partial A_{i}}{\partial x_{k}}
\end{eqnarray*}
\begin{equation*}
\dfrac{\partial\mathscr{L}}{\partial x_{i}}
\;\; = \;\;
    \dfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\!
    \left(\,
        \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,\mathbf{v}^2
        \, - \,
        q\,\phi(t,\mathbf{r})
        \, + \,
        q\,\mathbf{v} \bullet \mathbf{A}(t,\mathbf{r})
        \,\right)
\;\; = \;\;
    -\,q\,\dfrac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{i}}
    \, + \,
    q\cdot\overset{3}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}\;v_{k}\,\dfrac{\partial A_{k}}{\partial x_{i}}
\end{equation*}
Hence, the Euler-Lagrange equations give:
\begin{eqnarray*}
0
& = &
    \dfrac{\d}{\d\,t}\!\left(\,\dfrac{\partial\mathscr{L}}{\partial v_{i}}\,\right)
    \, - \,
    \dfrac{\partial\mathscr{L}}{\partial\,x_{i}}
\\
& = &
    \left(\,
        m\,\dot{v}_{i}
        \, + \,
        q\cdot\dfrac{\partial A_{i}}{\partial t}
        \, + \,
        q\cdot
        \overset{3}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}\;
        v_{k}
        \dfrac{\partial A_{i}}{\partial x_{k}}
        \,\right)
    \, - \,
    \left(\,
        -\,q\,\dfrac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{i}}
        \, + \,
        q\cdot\overset{3}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}\;v_{k}\,\dfrac{\partial A_{k}}{\partial x_{i}}
        \,\right)
\\
& = &
    m\,\dot{v}_{i}
    \, + \,
    q \cdot\!\left(\,
        \dfrac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{i}}
        \, + \,
        \dfrac{\partial A_{i}}{\partial t}
        \, + \,
        \overset{3}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}\;v_{k}\left(\,
            \dfrac{\partial A_{i}}{\partial x_{k}}
            \, - \,
            \dfrac{\partial A_{k}}{\partial x_{i}}
            \right)
        \right)
\end{eqnarray*}
Isolating the term \,$m\,\dot{v}_{i}$\, on one side yields
\begin{equation*}
m\,\dot{v}_{i}
\;\; = \;\;
    q \cdot\!\left(\,
        -\,
        \dfrac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{i}}
        \, - \,
        \dfrac{\partial A_{i}}{\partial t}
        \, + \,
        \overset{3}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}\;v_{k}\left(\,
            \dfrac{\partial A_{k}}{\partial x_{i}}
            \, - \,
            \dfrac{\partial A_{i}}{\partial x_{k}}
            \right)
        \right)
\end{equation*}
Recalling that
\,$\mathbf{F} \,=\, m\,\dot{\mathbf{v}}$\,
yields the desired expression of the Lorentz force, i.e.,
\begin{equation*}
\mathbf{F}
\;\; = \;\;
    q \cdot
    \left[\,
          -\, \nabla\phi
        \,-\, \dfrac{\partial\mathbf{A}}{\partial t}
        \,+\, \mathbf{v} \times (\nabla \times \mathbf{A})
        \,\right]
\end{equation*}
This completes the proof that the Euler-Lagrange equations
imply the explicit form of the Lorentz force.

\vskip 0.3cm
\noindent
Next, we derive the associated Hamiltonian.
The Hamiltonian is simply obtained from the Lagrangian via the Legendre transformation:
\begin{equation*}
\mathscr{H}
\;\; := \;\;
    \mathbf{v} \bullet \mathbf{p}
    \, - \,
    \mathscr{L}\,,
\end{equation*}
where
\,$\mathbf{p}$\,
is the \textit{canonical momentum} vector, whose components are defined by
\,$p_{i} \,:=\, \dfrac{\partial\mathscr{L}}{\partial v_{i}}$.\,
Hence,
\begin{equation*}
p_{i}
\;\; := \;\;
    \dfrac{\partial\mathscr{L}}{\partial v_{i}}
\;\; = \;\;
    \dfrac{\partial}{\partial v_{i}}\!
    \left(\,
        \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\cdot\Vert\,\mathbf{v}\,\Vert^2
        \, - \,
        q\cdot\phi
        \, + \,
        q\cdot\mathbf{v} \bullet \mathbf{A}
        \,\right)
\;\; = \;\;
    m\,v_{i}
    \, + \,
    q\,A_{i}
\end{equation*}
Equivalently, the canonical momentum vector \,$\mathbf{p}$\, is given by:
\begin{equation*}
\mathbf{p}
\;\; = \;\;
    m\cdot\mathbf{v}
    \, + \,
    q\cdot\mathbf{A}
\end{equation*}
Thus,
\begin{eqnarray*}
\mathscr{H}
& := &
    \mathbf{v} \bullet \mathbf{p}
    \, - \,
    \mathscr{L}
\;\; = \;\;
    m\cdot\Vert\,\mathbf{v}\,\Vert^{2}
    \, + \,
    q\cdot\mathbf{v} \bullet \mathbf{A}
    \, - \,
    \left(\,
        \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\cdot\Vert\,\mathbf{v}\,\Vert^2
        \, - \,
        q\cdot\phi
        \, + \,
        q\cdot\mathbf{v} \bullet \mathbf{A}
        \,\right)
\\
& = &
    \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\cdot\Vert\,\mathbf{v}\,\Vert^2
    \, + \,
    q\cdot\phi
\;\; = \;\;
    \dfrac{1}{2m}\,\Vert\,m\cdot\mathbf{v}\,\Vert^{2}
    \, + \,
    q\cdot\phi
\\
& = &
    \dfrac{1}{2m}\,\left\Vert\;
        \mathbf{p}
        \, \overset{{\color{white}.}}{-} \,
        q\cdot\mathbf{A}
        \,\right\Vert^{2}
    \, + \,
    q\cdot\phi\,,
\end{eqnarray*}
as required.
\qed

\vskip 0.5cm
\section{Pauli-Schr\"odinger equation -- via quantization of the Hamiltonian of the Lorenz force}

We will obtain the Pauli-Schr\"odinger equation by ``quantizing''
the Hamiltonian of the Lorentz force:
\begin{equation*}
\mathscr{H}(t, \mathbf{r}, \mathbf{v})
\;\; = \;\;
    \dfrac{1}{2m}\,\left\Vert\;
        \mathbf{p}(t,\mathbf{r},\mathbf{v})
        \, \overset{{\color{white}.}}{-} \,
        q\cdot\mathbf{A}(t,\mathbf{r})
        \,\right\Vert^{2}
    \, + \,
    q\cdot\phi(t,\mathbf{r})
\end{equation*}
``Quantization'' in the present context will mean
re-interpreting the quantities occurring in the
Lorentz-force Hamiltonian as (differential) operators
on spinor fields.
Recall that a spinor field is a smooth section of a spinor bundle,
i.e., a smooth section of a complex vector bundle associated
to a principal $\SU(2)$-bundle via a representation
\,$\rho : \SU(2) \xrightarrow{{\color{white}????}} U(\mathcal{H})$\,
on a (possibly infinite-dimensional) complex Hilbert space \,$\mathcal{H}$,\,
where \,$\rho$\, does NOT factor through the adjoint representation of \,$\SU(2)$:\,
\begin{equation*}
\Ad : \SU(2) \xrightarrow{{\color{white}2222}} \SO(\mathfrak{su}(2)) \cong \SO(3)
\end{equation*}

          %%%%% ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ %%%%%

\subsection{Some useful properties of the Pauli spin matrices}

\begin{definition}
\mbox{}
\vskip -0.01cm
\begin{enumerate}
\item
    The Pauli spin matrices:
    \begin{equation*}
    \sigma_{1}
    \, :=: \,
        \sigma_{x}
    \; := \;
        \left(\,\begin{array}{rr}
            0 & 1
            \\
            1 & 0
            \end{array}\,\right),
    \quad\quad
    \sigma_{2}
    \, :=: \,
        \sigma_{y}
    \; := \;
        \left(\,\begin{array}{rr}
            0 & -\,\i
            \\
            \i & 0
            \end{array}\,\right)
    \quad\quad
    \sigma_{3}
    \, :=: \,
        \sigma_{z}
    \; := \;
        \left(\,\begin{array}{rr}
            1 & 0
            \\
            0 & -1
            \end{array}\,\right)
    \end{equation*}
\item
    The ``vector'' \,$\boldsymbol{\sigma}$\, of Pauli spin matrices is, by definition,
    the ordered triple of the Pauli spin matrices, i.e.,
    \begin{equation*}
    \boldsymbol{\sigma}
    \;\; := \;\;
        \left(\, \sigma_{1} \,,\, \sigma_{2} \,,\, \sigma_{3} \,\right)
    \end{equation*}
\item
    The ``dot product''
    \,$\boldsymbol{\sigma} \bullet \mathbf{a}$\,
    of the vector
    \,$\boldsymbol{\sigma}$\,
    of Pauli spin matrices with a vector
    \,$\mathbf{a} \,=\, (a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}) \,\in\, \Re^{3}$\,
    is defined as follows:
    \begin{eqnarray*}
    \boldsymbol{\sigma} \bullet \mathbf{a}
    & := &
        \sigma_{1} \cdot a_{1}
        \, + \,
        \sigma_{2} \cdot a_{2}
        \, + \,
        \sigma_{3} \cdot a_{3}
    \;\; = \;\;
        \left(\,\begin{array}{rr}
            0 & 1
            \\
            1 & 0
            \end{array}\,\right)
        \cdot
        a_{1}
        \; + \;
        \left(\,\begin{array}{rr}
            0 & -\,\i
            \\
            \i & 0
            \end{array}\,\right)
        \cdot
        a_{2}
        \; + \;
        \left(\,\begin{array}{rr}
            1 & 0
            \\
            0 & -1
            \end{array}\,\right)
        \cdot
        a_{3}
    \\
    & = &
        \left(\,\begin{array}{cc}
            a_{3} & a_{1} - \i\,a_{2}
            \\
            a_{1} + \i\,a_{2} & -a_{3}
            \end{array}\,\right)
    \;\; \in \;\;
        \C^{2 \times 2}
    \end{eqnarray*}
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\vskip 0.5cm
\begin{lemma}
\mbox{}
\vskip 0.1cm
\noindent
Let
\,$\sigma_{1},\,\sigma_{2},\,\sigma_{3} \in \C^{2 \times 2}$\,
be the Pauli matrices and
\,$\boldsymbol{\sigma} \,=\, \left(\,\sigma_{1},\,\sigma_{2},\,\sigma_{3}\,\right)$\,
be the vector of Pauli matrices.
Then, the following identities are true:
\begin{enumerate}
\item
    $
    \sigma_{i} \cdot \sigma_{j}
    \, = \,
        \delta_{ij}\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{2 \times 2}
        \, + \,
        \i\cdot\varepsilon_{ij{\color{white}k}}^{{\color{white}ij}k}\cdot\sigma_{k}
    $,\,
    for each
    \,$i,j,k \in \{\,1,2,3\,\}$.\,
\item
    $
    \left(\,\boldsymbol{\sigma} \bullet \mathbf{a}\,\right)
    \cdot
    \left(\,\boldsymbol{\sigma} \bullet \mathbf{b}\,\right)
    \; = \;
        \left(\,\mathbf{a}\bullet\mathbf{b}\,\right)\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{2 \times 2}
        \, + \,
        \i\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma} \bullet \left(\,\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b}\,\right)
    \; \in \;
        \C^{2 \times 2}
    $,\,
    for each
    \,$\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b} \in \Re^{3}$.\,
\item
    $
    \left(\,\boldsymbol{\sigma} \bullet \mathbf{a}\,\right)^{2}
    \; = \;
        (\,\mathbf{a} \bullet \mathbf{a}\,)\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{2 \times 2}
    \; \in \;
        \C^{2 \times 2}
    $,\,
    for each
    \,$\mathbf{a} \in \Re^{3}$.\,
\item
    $
    \left(\,\boldsymbol{\sigma} \overset{{\color{white}1}}{\bullet} \widehat{\mathbf{a}}\,\right)
    \!\cdot\!
    \left(\,\boldsymbol{\sigma} \bullet \widehat{\mathbf{b}}\,\right)
    \; = \;
        \left(\,\widehat{\mathbf{a}}\bullet\widehat{\mathbf{b}}\,\right)
        \, + \,
        \i\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma} \bullet \left(\,\widehat{\mathbf{a}} \times \widehat{\mathbf{b}}\,\right)
    \; \in \;
        U\!\left(\,L^{2}(\Re^{3})\overset{{\color{white}.}}{\otimes}\C^{2}\,\right)
    $,\,
    for arbitrary ordered triples
    \,$\widehat{\mathbf{a}} = \left(\,\widehat{a}_{1},\,\widehat{a}_{2},\,\widehat{a}_{3}\,\right)$\,
    and
    \,$\widehat{\mathbf{b}} = \left(\,\widehat{b}_{1},\,\widehat{b}_{2},\,\widehat{b}_{3}\,\right)$\,
    of operators on $L^{2}(\Re^{3})$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\proof
\begin{enumerate}
\item
    We compute:
    \begin{equation*}
    \begin{array}{ccccccrcc}
    \sigma_{1} \cdot \sigma_{1}
    & = &
        \left(\,\begin{array}{rr}
            0 & 1
            \\
            1 & 0
            \end{array}\,\right)
        \cdot
        \left(\,\begin{array}{rr}
            0 & 1
            \\
            1 & 0
            \end{array}\,\right)
    & = &
        \left(\,\begin{array}{rr}
            1 & 0
            \\
            0 & 1
            \end{array}\,\right)
    & = &
        \boldsymbol{1}_{2 \times 2}
    & = &
        \delta_{11}\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{2 \times 2}
        \, + \,
        \i\cdot\varepsilon_{11{\color{white}k}}^{{\color{white}11}k}\cdot\sigma_{k}
    \\
    \sigma_{1} \cdot \sigma_{2}
    & = &
        \left(\,\begin{array}{rr}
            0 & 1
            \\
            1 & 0
            \end{array}\,\right)
        \cdot
        \left(\,\begin{array}{rr}
            0 & -\,\i
            \\
            \i & 0
            \end{array}\,\right)
    & = &
        \left(\,\begin{array}{rr}
            \i & 0
            \\
            0 & -\i
            \end{array}\,\right)
    & = &
        \i\cdot\sigma_{3}
    & = &
        \delta_{12}\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{2 \times 2}
        \, + \,
        \i\cdot\varepsilon_{12{\color{white}k}}^{{\color{white}12}k}\cdot\sigma_{k}
    \\
    \sigma_{1} \cdot \sigma_{3}
    & = &
        \left(\,\begin{array}{rr}
            0 & 1
            \\
            1 & 0
            \end{array}\,\right)
        \cdot
        \left(\,\begin{array}{rr}
            1 & 0
            \\
            0 & -1
            \end{array}\,\right)
    & = &
        \left(\,\begin{array}{rr}
            0 & -1
            \\
            1 & 0
            \end{array}\,\right)
    & = &
        -\,\i\cdot\sigma_{2}
    & = &
        \delta_{13}\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{2 \times 2}
        \, + \,
        \i\cdot\varepsilon_{13{\color{white}k}}^{{\color{white}12}k}\cdot\sigma_{k}
    \end{array}
    \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*}
    \begin{array}{ccccccrcc}
    \sigma_{2} \cdot \sigma_{1}
    & = &
        \left(\,\begin{array}{rr}
            0 & -\i
            \\
            \i & 0
            \end{array}\,\right)
